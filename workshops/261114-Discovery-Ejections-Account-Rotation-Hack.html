02Concept Review

A · Discovery: the market looking for new prices

Most of the time the market is in balance, trading around fair value. In discovery, it leaves that balance area to look for new prices. You can usually spot it when price is outside 1 standard deviation from VWAP and keeps moving that way with few pullbacks. If price pokes outside the band and snaps back, that's still balance. Trades taken in the direction of discovery have a higher chance of working. When the market isn't clearly moving one way, be more careful.

The big takeaway: when the market is in discovery, you have several good ways to get in with the trend:

  • SwPt breaks: price breaks a swing point in the direction of the trend.
  • Gap confirmations: a gap forms in the direction of the trend and holds.
  • Pullbacks: price pulls back toward a level, then continues in the direction of the trend.
What it isPrice leaving the current balance area to find new value.
How to spot itOutside 1 SD of VWAP, moving consistently in one direction, few pullbacks.
How to applySwPt breaks, gap confirmations and pullbacks in the direction of the trend are all good trades. Don't fade it just because it looks "too far."

B · Ejections: what it takes to switch direction

An ejection is when the market moves away from a key level hard enough to leave gaps behind, and then follows through, moving further away in the direction of the gap. A gap on its own isn't an ejection: it takes the gap plus the follow-through. That displacement shows one side is pushing back at the level. The rule: you need at least two ejections before changing your directional bias. One ejection is only a warning. The second ejection doesn't have to come from the same level. Most trend changes do retrace back to the level before the second ejection (first diagram). But sometimes the market takes off in a tear, pauses, and makes a second displacement away from the pause. That also signals a reversal (second diagram). In the session example, the market reached buy-side liquidity and ejected with bearish gaps. Then it ejected a second time with about 3× normal volume, and that second ejection moved the focus to shorts.

C · The account rotation hack

Instead of putting every trade through one account, rotate trades across your funded accounts, one trade per account at a time, with risk capped around $250 per trade. A rough session gets spread across several drawdown limits instead of hitting one, so any single account is less likely to hit max drawdown that day. Each account also lasts longer, long enough for the right market to show up. It's not a replacement for learning to trade. Practice in the simulator first. A 37%+ win rate is a good benchmark that you're trading consistently.

D · Position size comes from the stop, not your gut

Decide your dollar risk first. Then let the stop distance tell you how many contracts to trade. A wide stop like the bear-gap setup (about 9–10.5 points) means fewer contracts. If the setup confirms, you can add to the position, as long as the total risk stays under your cap.

E · News candles and the report rule

The 8:30 report candle had long wicks on both sides. That means buyers and sellers are still fighting it out, so chasing it is a coin flip. Swing-point pullbacks are the core of how we trade. Let price come back to a level you've already marked, then look for confirmation, like the hammer reversal from the long example.

F · The funded-account path

Treat the funded stage as building a cushion, not hitting a jackpot. Once your $2,000 buffer is in place, set a daily target of about $500 plus $2 per contract traded, and stop opening new trades when you reach it. Many firms require 5 winning days per payout cycle and cap each payout at $2,000. Profit you keep in the account grows your buffer before you move to a live account.

03Common Mistakes Covered

  • Chasing a volatile report candle. Wait for the pullback.
  • Calling it discovery when price pokes outside the band and snaps back. Look for consistent one-way movement with few pullbacks.
  • Running every trade through one account and hitting max drawdown in one bad session. Rotate across accounts.
  • Going full size before the setup confirms. Start small and add on confirmation.
  • Flipping your bias after a single ejection. Wait for at least two.
  • Trading trend-line scalps with real money before you understand them. They move fast, so practice in sim first.
  • Paying monthly for an order-routing tool before you're getting consistent payouts. Use the separate bracket-order workaround for now.

04Platform Tip

TradingView's order-entry levels don't work reliably with the firm's Rithmic data feed. Until that's fixed, put in separate bracketed orders for each piece of your position. It's clunky, but each piece keeps its own stop and target.
